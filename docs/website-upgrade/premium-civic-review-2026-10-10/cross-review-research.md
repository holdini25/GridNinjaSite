## Offer, civic narrative and operating authority

The written design keeps the current commercial action a bounded, paid assessment using authorized historical inputs. It retains the two approved CTA labels, explicitly says there is no live connection/equipment control, and distinguishes scoping from paid agreement. The civic mission is introduced as **Our ambition**, then immediately connected to what GridNinja offers today. Grid-service delivery, local-impact studies and community-benefit claims require separate scope, evidence and authority; they are not silently added to assessment deliverables.

The proposed relationship drawing treats the grid as shared infrastructure and the community as outside the site boundary. Its specification excludes arrows, energized homes, export meters, heat-delivery networks, benefit counters and customer-like place names. Its caption states that no live connection, grid service or community benefit is demonstrated. These are sound instructions for avoiding an implied operating capability; visual interpretation still needs checking on the eventual rendered artwork.

About-page copy explicitly declines to establish community acceptance or public benefit. Bridge-power copy raises fuel/noise/emissions/water/reserve questions without claiming clean generation or savings. The authority diagram separates a model finding, human/business review and operating permission, while the surrounding proof text preserves the fuller distinctions among evidence, acceptance and authority. These choices protect the product narrative while allowing an accountable-neighbor ambition to be visible.

The specification retains synthetic status, unestimated economics and accepted/delivered capacity being not applicable. It requires selector-backed values and protects immutable publication bytes, downloads and access states. No proposed source can be used as evidence of GridNinja capacity, customer outcomes, water savings, household-bill reductions or autonomous safety.

## Research and bibliography checks

- The three homepage IDs are consistent and ordered as field demonstration, modeled emissions tradeoff, and local water context. Their source-specific limitations remain mandatory and visible; the general footer cannot replace them.
- The seven-paper reading list refers to published journal versions. Nature Energy uses the 2026 volume with a 2025 online date; IEEE uses the 2023 volume with a 2022 online date. The heat-reuse article's four authors are confirmed against its title page and bibliographic record.
- The grid paper's cluster result remains external; the system-cost paper is not converted into a household-price claim; the water paper is not presented as a current inventory. German stated preferences are not treated as U.S. consent, Finnish heat-system modeling is not a shipped heat service, and control-theory simulations are not data-center certification.
- Exact iScience and Automatica initial online dates remain unverified in primary metadata and are represented as unknown. Accurate journal/year citations remain available. Access and review-depth limitations are preserved; neither full-text audit nor independent reproduction is claimed.
- The catalog is a documentation contract example, not imported site content. It sets `gridNinjaOutcomeEvidence: false` and `figureReuseApproved: false` for every record.
